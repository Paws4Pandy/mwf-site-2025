Mortgage Basics Guide

Your Essential Introduction to Canadian Home Financing

Welcome to homeownership! This guide covers the essential mortgage basics every Canadian homebuyer should know. Whether you're a first-time buyer or looking to refresh your knowledge, this comprehensive overview will help you navigate the mortgage process with confidence.

1. What is a Mortgage?

A mortgage is a loan secured by real estate property. When you get a mortgage, you're borrowing money to purchase a home, and the property itself serves as collateral for the loan.

Key Components:

  • Principal: The amount you borrow
  • Interest: The cost of borrowing money
  • Amortization: The time it takes to pay off the loan (usually 25-30 years)
  • Term: The length of your current mortgage agreement (typically 1-5 years)

2. Down Payment Requirements

In Canada, your down payment requirement depends on the home's purchase price:

Down Payment Rules (2024):

  • Homes under $500,000: Minimum 5%
  • Homes $500,000-$999,999: 5% on first $500K + 10% on remainder
  • Homes $1M+: Minimum 20%

Pro Tip: A larger down payment means lower monthly payments and potentially better interest rates. You'll also avoid mortgage default insurance if you put down 20% or more.

3. Types of Mortgages

Fixed vs. Variable Rate

Fixed Rate: Your interest rate stays the same for the entire term. Provides payment certainty but may be higher than variable rates.

Variable Rate: Your rate fluctuates with the prime rate. Often starts lower but can increase over time.

Open vs. Closed Mortgages

Closed Mortgage: Lower rates but limited prepayment options. Most common choice.

Open Mortgage: Higher rates but maximum flexibility to pay off early without penalties.

4. The Mortgage Application Process

Documents You'll Need:

  • Employment letter and recent pay stubs
  • 2 years of tax returns and notices of assessment
  • 3 months of bank statements
  • Investment statements
  • Identification (driver's license, passport)
  • Details of existing debts

Steps in the Process:

  • Pre-approval: Get approved for a specific amount
  • House hunting: Shop within your approved budget
  • Firm approval: Final approval based on chosen property
  • Home inspection: Ensure property condition
  • Legal review: Lawyer reviews all documents
  • Closing: Transfer of ownership and funding

5. Understanding Mortgage Costs

Beyond Your Down Payment:

  • Land Transfer Tax: Provincial tax on property transfer
  • Legal Fees: Lawyer or notary costs ($1,500-$3,000)
  • Home Inspection: $400-$700
  • Appraisal: $300-$500 (if required)
  • Moving Costs: Variable
  • Mortgage Default Insurance: If down payment < 20%

6. Improving Your Mortgage Application

Before You Apply:

  • Check and improve your credit score
  • Pay down existing debts
  • Save for a larger down payment
  • Gather all required documents
  • Avoid major purchases or credit applications
